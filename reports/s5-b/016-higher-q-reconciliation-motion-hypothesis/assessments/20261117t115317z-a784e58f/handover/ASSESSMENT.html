Decision

The document reconciliation is complete for all declared references. The recovered records establish useful positive static results for Q22 and Q17/Q19, alongside a negative Q18 hyperbolic-reproduction result. They do not establish higher-Q motion readiness or electron properties. The earlier Q2–Q16 allocation closure and original gate failures remain intact.

Do not repeat the old Q22 static reproduction to demonstrate something already measured. Replace the provisional two-arm, 400-step Q22 proposal with a narrower compatibility question: can an authenticated successful static field meet the current D4 action's requirements at declared resolution? The next numerical work should answer that question on an existing control before expanding Q17–Q32.

Why later preparation failures do not erase these results

The old static benchmark uses the cell/Gauss functional and an unweighted tangent-gradient RMS criterion below 0.001. The later physical-time scheme uses a different D4 lattice action and strict relative-force/maximum-acceleration criteria. They share a declared continuum model, not identical finite-lattice stationary points or interchangeable residual definitions. An old gate pass is neither a current D4 pass nor retrospectively invalidated by failure under another lattice action.

The later Q17/Q19 screen additionally sampled original 129-cubed, spacing-0.12, side-15.36 fields onto a 97-cubed, spacing-0.24, side-23.04 grid. Spacing and box both changed. After four 50-step readmission calls, relative forces were 0.00113764 and 0.00218160, and maximum accelerations 0.0258645 and 0.0222044. Their moving arms did not run. Those results cannot distinguish action mismatch, representation changes and incomplete relaxation, and cannot demonstrate moving-knot instability. The current readiness gates remain unchanged.

The neutral admission assessment also found that integrated stress changed substantially with domain even when energy changed little. At fixed spacing 0.096, increasing full side from 15.36 to 18.432 reduced normalized stress by 77.19% for Q17, 62.90% for the Q18 baseline and 70.08% for Q19. Small energy changes alone are insufficient evidence for isolated-particle dynamics. The larger-box fields were separately relaxed, so this is not a unique decomposition of boundary versus residual/basin effects.

Motion-dependent stability

The completed internal-rotation diagnostic reduces Q2's RMS residual by 3.7903% and peak by 2.4085%. Q8's RMS reduction is about 0.000000748%, while its peak worsens by 0.001194%. There is no useful support for reopening Q8 through this ansatz or expanding its frequency scan.

This does not determine existence or stability of all relative equilibria or periodic configurations. A genuinely time-dependent branch needs its own consistent equations, localization/radiation and perturbation-growth tests; static stationarity is not a universal exclusion test for such a branch. Conversely, assigning spin or velocity to an unresolved field is not evidence of stabilization. No numerical likelihood of motion-dependent stability can be justified from this evidence.

Recommended next work

1. Record the reconciled assessment and preserve the original Q2–Q16 closure. Mark the Q22 bounded static benchmark and Q17/Q19 selected static branches as supported with their historical limits; retain Q18's failed hyperbolic reproduction and all later unresolved D4 preparations. The present result is an assessment for subsequent workstation publication, not an update already made to GitHub.

2. Use one authenticated higher-Q field for a cell-to-D4 compatibility test. First evaluate the native field on its existing grid and box, without relaxation, using both operators and explicitly labelled metric definitions. Include the retained Q2 instrument control. Do not resample and alter the box at this first stage. Historical compact records cannot substitute for the required actual field bytes.

3. Q17 is the practical first control if the Q22 checkpoint is unavailable: the recovered archive guide documents a verified full-field archive containing the exact original Q17/Q19 source fields. Restore only through the workstation into a new directory and verify field hashes. It is not proof that the older Q22/Q18 checkpoints have been archived; their restoration remains a separate availability question. Prefer a verified native Q22 field as a cable regression when available, rather than regenerating a new seed and calling it historical continuation.

4. Only after that native comparison, allocate bounded D4 readmission with fixed grid, box, action and gates. Change one numerical factor at a time. Sample more densely around residual turns or acceleration spikes; compare measured joint force/acceleration progress and energy descent. Linear, low-order polynomial or exponential fits can guide observations and conditional continuation, with out-of-window checks; a predicted threshold crossing is not a measured admission pass. A fixed maximum budget and retained checkpoints must precede execution.

5. Run motion only after current representation, stationarity and moving-boundary admission. Use a short, controlled trajectory and time-step comparison before a long run. Retain Q18 hyperbolic-basin work as secondary, Q8 rotation rescue as unsupported, and the broad Q17–Q32 detailed campaign as deferred. The existing static successes justify a focused bridge, not a forecast that a larger Q survey will produce an electron model.

The charged-model equation audit has already been conducted. It identified finite-core observable, general Maxwell-equivalence and evolution limitations in that candidate. Repeating the same audit or finding another neutral knot does not resolve those physical gates. The project has useful numerical benchmarks, but an accepted charged, stable, moving electron configuration remains unestablished.
