# Controlled questions and interpretation

The previous diagnosis reproduced Q2 and found that over 99.999% of squared force residual in both current Q21 and Q22 endpoints lies outside the three-unit boundary guard. This motivates an interior preparation diagnostic. It does not prove an optimizer bug, rule out nonlocal boundary effects, or make the boundary-energy failures disappear.

Q21 is the primary controlled test because its force numerator genuinely improved in the retained continuation. The exact iteration-295 optimizer state, including old gradient, preconditioned gradient, direction and alpha, is authenticated independently of the terminal field. Both arms start from those same arrays and scalars. Only the reset arm clears the three conjugacy arrays. The inherited projected Sobolev-CG algorithm, 45-trial Armijo policy, periodic reset schedule and all registered measurement gates remain unchanged. No periodic optimizer reset falls between iterations 295 and 315.

Before those updates, constrained finite differences check the D4 energy gradient in two tangent directions with centered epsilon values 0.001, 0.0003 and 0.0001. Both smallest epsilon comparisons must agree within the predeclared absolute-plus-relative tolerance for both directions. Failure defers both optimizer arms. These evaluations are not accepted optimizer steps and do not change the original field.

Every accepted update is measured. Representation loss, line-search failure, a measured strict-stationarity result or exhaustion of 20 charged attempts stops an arm. Boundary admission is reported at every point and remains an independent required condition; an already failing boundary fraction does not prohibit this explicitly labelled diagnostic. No field can be promoted to motion readiness by ignoring a failed boundary gate. A resource stop preserves committed checkpoints and charged attempts.

Compare arms only at common measured iterations. Report initial, intermediate and endpoint behaviour rather than selecting a favourable isolated point. Objective calls distinguish an inexpensive update from an expensive line search. Linear, quadratic and exponential descriptions reserve the final three points as chronological holdout; coefficients are re-fitted afterward for reporting. Fits cannot increase the budget, change gates, imply a measured threshold crossing or justify a motion run. Dense per-update observations already resolve every available optimizer step; there is no skipped interval around a turn.

Historical Q22 addresses a different question: whether the exact formerly successful native cell-functional checkpoint is also stationary and boundary-admitted under the D4 action on that same native grid and box. The current fresh cable-family preparation is not that checkpoint. The source hash, spacing and node count must match. Use the CPU cell/Gauss kernel to report its unweighted interior tangent-gradient RMS and original energy, and separately report the D4 relative-force, maximum-acceleration, topology, unit-error and registered continuum-density tail/guard fractions. These are distinct finite-lattice definitions, not interchangeable thresholds.

The archival Q22 NPZ may hold float32 values even though optimization was performed at float64. Promote stored values exactly to float64 for operator evaluation without renormalizing them; retain measured unit error and the stored dtype. This tests the authenticated archival field, not a reconstructed full-precision solver state. Differences from the historical solver metrics are retained for review and must not be automatically attributed to rounding alone. Missing exact field bytes produce explicit deferral rather than a regenerated substitute or withdrawal of earlier positive findings.

The campaign takes no physical-time steps and makes no global-minimum, continuum-isotopy, long-time stability or electron-property claim. Q2/Q3 controls and all historical cell-functional positive results remain in their original scope. Only further assessment of the measured outcomes may justify another numerical allocation.
