# Purpose and scope

## Research question

Can field topology, full electromagnetic field structure and energy distribution, under explicitly stated relativistic dynamics, produce stable localised configurations whose interactions reproduce properties attributed to electrons?

The motivating suggestion is not that changing a wave's outline directly changes charge. It is that a structured distribution of fields and energy might generate charge-like behaviour when interacting with other systems. Energy density alone does not specify field direction or phase; models must specify the full state.

## Objectives

1. Build a transparent computational laboratory with analytical and numerical benchmarks.
2. Reproduce known Maxwell knots and separate topology persistence from energy confinement.
3. Quantify energy, momentum, angular momentum, electric flux and localisation.
4. Explore valid field families systematically, within the constraints of their equations.
5. Assess explicit extensions only where the baseline cannot support the proposed property.
6. Investigate interactions and later quantum compatibility of any surviving candidates.
7. Publish reproducible positive, negative and inconclusive outcomes.

## Scope boundaries

Initially: classical vacuum Maxwell fields on flat, ordinary three-dimensional space evolving in time. No nucleus is required. Existing electron observations, including isolated-electron spin, constrain later models.

Later, conditional on justified equations: nonlinear or additional-field theories, changed spatial topology, and mathematical quantum analysis. A different theory must not be described as an ordinary Maxwell result.

Excluded from the initial programme: constructing a material detector, claims to replace QED, experimental particle apparatus, speculative fitting of all particle masses, paid compute and exhaustive searches of all knots.

## Evidence ladder

A valid mathematical configuration is not automatically a stable dynamical solution.
A stable solution is not automatically an electron.
Agreement with selected observations is not experimental confirmation.
A complete proposal must address electric charge, invariant mass, spin and statistics, magnetic response, scattering and appropriate quantum interactions.

The useful outcome may be a constraint, a reproducible benchmark or a rejected class of models rather than a new electron theory.
