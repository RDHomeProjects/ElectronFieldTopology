## Definitions and equation-to-code map

Let r2=x*x+y*y+z*z, D=r2-(t-i)^2, A=r2-t*t-1+2*i*z, C=2*(x-i*y).
Take alpha=A/D, beta=C/D with p=q=1, and F=E+iB=grad(alpha) cross grad(beta).

| Source | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Eq. (1), F=E+iB | fields.hopfion returns real and imaginary parts |
| Eq. (2), cross product of gradients | numpy.cross of analytic quotient-rule gradients |
| Eq. (4), p=1 | alpha numerator A and denominator D |
| Eq. (5), q=1 | beta numerator C and denominator D |
| Derived diagnostics | diagnostics.py; energy and Poynting flow from E/B |

With X=(x,y,z), e_z=(0,0,1):

```text
grad(alpha) = [(2X+2i e_z)D - 2X A] / D^2
grad(beta)  = [(2,-2i,0)D - 2X C] / D^2
```

All inputs are real spacetime coordinates; D has no real-spacetime zeros (its imaginary part forces t=0, where D=r2+1).

## Separately reduced Cartesian reference

With a=x-i*y and b=t-z-i:

```text
F = (4(b^2-a^2), -4i(a^2+b^2), 8ab) / D^3
```

The tests evaluate this polynomial form separately from the quotient-gradient implementation. Exact symbolic differentiation of the polynomial gives div(F)=0 and dt(F)+i curl(F)=0, verifying both divergence constraints and both Maxwell curl equations. This is an independent algebraic check of the same mathematical solution, not a second independent physical theory.

At t=0 the independent radial reduction is:

```text
u(r) = (|E|^2+|B|^2)/2 = 16/(1+r^2)^4
U_all_space = 64*pi*integral_0^infinity r^2/(1+r^2)^4 dr = 2*pi^2
E(0,0) = (-4,0,0); B(0,0) = (0,4,0)
```

Sphere quadrature is compared with a separately evaluated one-dimensional radial integral. Finite-radius missing energy is not a conservation failure.

## Domain, units and normalisation

Unbounded ordinary flat space, no sources, singularities, mirrors or internal boundaries. Finite spheres and plotted grids are sampling regions, not imposed evolution boundaries. Unit dimensionless length and amplitude; c=epsilon_0=mu_0=1. No physical length, electron mass, Planck constant or elementary charge is fitted or predicted.

For optional SI interpretation choose l0 and electric-field scale E0 explicitly:
x_phys=l0*x, t_phys=l0*t/c, E_phys=E0*E, B_phys=(E0/c)*B.
Energy units are epsilon_0*E0^2*l0^3 and electric-flux charge units epsilon_0*E0*l0^2. Q_flux remains zero regardless of those arbitrary scales.
No four-tensor, gauge potential or metric convention is used here; future Lorentz/gauge implementations must declare theirs.

## Diagnostics and acceptance

Energy, Poynting vector, P, J about the coordinate origin, energy centroid and sphere electric flux. Quadrature: Gauss-Legendre radial/cos(theta) nodes, uniform periodic azimuth nodes. Localisation spheres are fixed at origin and at the analytical centre trajectory (0,0,-t/2); finite-domain centroids are also measured. Their agreement is a diagnostic, not a boost test.

Before the numerical run the tests prescribed: polynomial agreement within 2e-13 relative/2e-14 absolute; normalised null-invariant errors <1e-13; second-order residual ratios 3.5..4.5 at h=.04,.02,.01 and finest maximum <.01; off-centre surface flux <2e-11 at high quadrature order; t=0 radial quadrature error <1e-10. These are benchmark tolerances, not experimental precision claims.

Symbolic tests give exact zeros; sampled residuals have finite-difference truncation and floating-point error. Additional tests check localisation quadrature convergence. Topology is inherited from the selected published solution; no knot invariant has been computed and no 3D field-line topology verification is claimed.

## Limits

This evaluates known fields at chosen times. It does not independently propagate initial data, demonstrate perturbation stability, introduce nonlinear binding, derive electric charge, establish quantum spin or model an electron. Those remain later research stages.
